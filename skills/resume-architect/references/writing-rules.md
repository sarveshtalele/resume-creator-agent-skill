# Writing Rules

Everything on the page competes for one screen of a recruiter's attention and one pass of a parser. These rules decide what earns a line.

## The bullet formula

```
[Outcome verb] [what changed] [how / with what] [quantified result]
```

Weak: `Responsible for improving the data pipeline.`
Strong: `Rebuilt the nightly ingestion pipeline in Go with incremental checkpoints, cutting load time from 6h to 48m for 40M daily records.`

Constraints:

- One line at 10.5pt is roughly 104 characters. Target 120–200 characters so a bullet wraps to two lines at most.
- At least half of all bullets carry a number. Percentages count when absolutes are confidential.
- Lead with the outcome when the outcome is the impressive part; lead with the scope when the scale is.
- No first-person pronouns, no articles at the start, no period-ending fragments mixed with full sentences — pick one and hold it.
- Present tense for the current role, past tense everywhere else.

## Verbs

Build: architected, built, designed, engineered, implemented, launched, migrated, rebuilt, shipped.
Improve: accelerated, automated, consolidated, cut, optimised, reduced, streamlined, tightened.
Grow: doubled, expanded, grew, increased, scaled, unlocked.
Lead: coordinated, directed, drove, led, mentored, onboarded, owned, partnered.
Analyse: audited, diagnosed, forecast, instrumented, measured, modelled, traced.
Secure: hardened, isolated, patched, remediated, safeguarded.

Banned openers: responsible for, worked on, helped with, assisted in, participated in, involved in, tasked with, successfully, various.

## Keyword integration

1. Take the must-have list extracted from the posting.
2. Place each keyword where it is **true**: the tool goes in the bullet describing the work it was used for; the domain noun goes in the summary; the certification goes in Certifications.
3. Skills section carries the remainder — grouped, comma-separated, no proficiency bars or ratings.
4. Use the posting's exact wording for the tool ("Amazon Redshift" versus "Redshift") once, then the natural short form afterwards.
5. Never repeat a keyword more than three times across the document. Stuffing reads as spam to humans and adds nothing to modern parsers.
6. A keyword with no real experience behind it does not go on the page. Say so in the plan instead, and note the gap for the cover letter.

## Truthfulness gate

Every line traces back to one of: the LinkedIn export, an answer given during intake, or a document the candidate supplied. If a number cannot be traced, it does not ship. When a candidate offers a range, write the conservative end. When a title on record differs from the work performed, keep the recorded title and let the bullets carry the scope.

## The summary

Three sentences at most:

1. Identity — target title, years, domain.
2. Proof — signature achievement with a number.
3. Fit — two or three must-have keywords, phrased as capability.

Cut every adjective that no one would claim the opposite of. Nobody advertises being an unmotivated poor communicator, so "motivated, detail-oriented team player with excellent communication skills" carries zero information and burns two lines.

## Skills section

- 3–5 labelled rows, 4–8 items each.
- Group by category the posting would recognise: Languages, Frameworks, Cloud & Infrastructure, Data, Tooling, Domain.
- Hard skills only. Leadership and communication belong in bullets where they are demonstrated.
- Order items by relevance to the posting, not alphabetically.

## Trimming to one page, in order

1. Cut bullets from the oldest role, down to one line each.
2. Remove roles older than 12–15 years, or collapse them into an `Additional Experience` one-liner.
3. Tighten each remaining bullet to a single line by removing preamble clauses.
4. Reduce the summary to two sentences.
5. Merge two skills rows.
6. Drop optional sections: Projects for senior candidates, Publications when the posting is not research-facing.

Never: shrink below 10pt, cut margins under 0.4in, drop dates, remove the contact line, or use a two-column layout to buy space.

## What never goes on the page

Photos, logos, rating bars, icons, tables, text boxes, headers and footers, street addresses, birth date, marital status, national identity numbers, salary history, references, "References available on request", or a personal pronoun.
